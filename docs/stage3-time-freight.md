# Stage 3: monthly trend, freight share and review score vs lateness

Builds on the stage 1 database (`data/processed/olist.db`). All three tables
count **delivered orders only**, matching stage 2. Generated by
`python src/build_time_freight.py`; verified by 14 independent tests in
`tests/test_time_freight.py` that recompute every figure from the database
in plain Python (34 tests pass in total with stage 1).

## monthly_revenue_trend.csv

Revenue (item prices, freight excluded), order count, items sold and average
order value per purchase month.

Headline findings:

- Steep growth through 2017: from BRL 111,798 in 2017-01 to a peak of
  **BRL 987,765 in 2017-11** (7,289 orders), the Black Friday month, after
  which 2017-12 drops to BRL 726,033.
- 2018 runs at a high plateau of roughly BRL 830k-980k per month
  (peak 2018-05, BRL 977,545).
- Average order value sits around BRL 135-153 in the mature months; the
  Black Friday peak month is at the low end (BRL 135.51).
- Edge months are artefacts of the dataset window, not real demand:
  2016-09 has one order, there is no 2016-11 row at all, and the dataset
  ends after 2018-08.

## freight_share_by_state.csv

Freight as a share of total order value (items + freight) per customer
state, sorted by that share.

Headline findings:

- Remote northern states pay the most for shipping relative to order value:
  **RR 21.9%**, MA 20.8%, RO 19.8%, AM 19.7%.
- **SP, the largest market (40,501 delivered orders), pays the least at
  12.2%** - consistent with sellers and carriers being concentrated in the
  southeast.
- The spread between the cheapest and most expensive state is nearly 10
  percentage points, so freight policy is a real competitive lever outside
  the southeast.

## review_score_vs_lateness.csv

Average review score by how late the delivery was against its estimated
date. Reviews are deduplicated as distinct (review_id, order_id) pairs, the
stage 2 convention; an order with more than one review contributes each
pair once. "On time or early" means delivered on or before the estimated
date (ISO text comparison, see `src/build_dataset.py`).

Headline findings:

- **92.0% of reviews are for on-time or early deliveries**, averaging
  **4.29 stars**.
- Reviews drop sharply once late: 3.18 stars at 1-7 days late, and
  **1.75/1.62 stars at 8-14 / 15-30 days late**.
- The "over 30 days late" bucket averages 2.02, slightly above the 8-30
  day buckets - a small sample (345 reviews, 0.36%), so treat it as noise,
  not recovery.
- Practical read: missing the promised date by more than a week is what
  destroys the rating, not being a few days late.
